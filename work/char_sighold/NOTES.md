# sg13g2_hv_sighold characterization

Generated by `work/char_sighold.py`. The cell is a bus holder: two
cross-coupled inverters on one `inout` pin. It has **no timing arcs** —
the thin-oxide model is `driver_type : bus_hold` plus capacitance and
per-state leakage, and nothing else — so no delay characterization is
needed or meaningful.

## Results

| quantity | value | thin-oxide counterpart |
|---|---|---|
| area | 20.5632 µm² | 9.072 µm² |
| leakage, `SH` (high) | 0.011793 nW | 138.903 nW |
| leakage, `!SH` (low) | 0.022442 nW | 184.238 nW |
| pin capacitance | 0.003670 pF | 0.0182 pF (see below) |

Leakage is ~10⁴× below the thin-oxide cell. That is the expected thick-
oxide result and matches the rest of this library: the tie cells measure
0.011/0.013 nW against 58 nW for their thin-oxide counterparts, the same
ratio, for the same reason (0.45–0.70 µm channels and a thick gate oxide
instead of 0.13 µm).

## Method

**Leakage** — `SH` held at each rail by an ideal source at exactly
VDD/0 V, so the keeper carries no steady-state current through the
driving source and `i(VDD)` is the true subthreshold path (off PMOS into
the internal node, sunk by the off NMOS). Settled-tail average over
40–60 ns, the same deck style as `work/tie_leakage.py` and
`work/seq_leakage.py`.

**Capacitance** — 1 MHz small-signal AC, `C = Im(I)/(2πf)`, averaged over
four bias points near the rails (0.05, 0.30, 3.00, 3.25 V). The result is
flat across bias — 0.00377 / 0.00378 / 0.00353 / 0.00361 pF, a 7 % spread
— which is what a physical capacitance should look like. Mid-rail is
deliberately excluded: the cross-coupled pair has gain there and the
small-signal result is not meaningful.

Charge integration (the method used for ordinary gate pins in
`work/fo4.py` and `work/finalize_lib.py`) was tried first and **rejected**
for this cell. On a bus holder the integral is dominated by the keeper
fighting the driver, so it is not a property of the cell but of whatever
drives it — measured rise charge against driving edge rate:

| driving edge | 0.2 ns | 0.5 ns | 1 ns | 2 ns | 5 ns |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| rise charge | 30.5 fC | 38.1 fC | 46.3 fC | 58.4 fC | 86.4 fC |

A monotone 2.8× spread over the range. Reporting any single point as
`capacitance` would bake an arbitrary driver assumption into the library.
This also explains the thin-oxide cell's 2.8× rise/fall asymmetry
(0.0268 vs 0.0096 pF): that asymmetry is a fight-charge artifact of its
measurement, not a property of the structure. The HV cell therefore ships
equal `rise_capacitance` and `fall_capacitance`.

**Keeper fight-back charge**, measured separately and recorded here
rather than folded into `capacitance`: at a 1 ns driving edge, 46.3 fC
rising and 32.7 fC falling. A driver overriding this holder must supply
that on top of the pin capacitance. The cell carries `dont_use` and
`dont_touch`, so it is never inserted automatically — it is placed
deliberately, by a designer who is choosing to override it.

## Functional check

Drive-then-release, with the released node isolated by 1 TΩ: `SH` holds
**3.30000 V** after being driven high and released, and **2.6e-08 V**
after being driven low and released. Both states hold rail-to-rail.

## Deliberate omission

No `internal_power`. The HV library ships **zero** switching-power tables
for any cell (`internal_power`, `rise_power`, `passive_POWER*` all have 0
occurrences); characterizing power for this cell alone would make
library-wide power analysis inconsistent — every other cell would report
zero switching power while this one did not. Documented as a library-level
limitation instead.
