**Flagged, and not a defect in the data.**  This arc is measured with a
different criterion from whatever produced the thin-oxide numbers, so the
2.66x factor does not apply to it and the ratio is reported rather than tuned.
See the `three_state_disable` section of `NOTES.md` for the full argument; in
short, a voltage-threshold measurement of a capacitively loaded Hi-Z node is
dominated by the keeper (0.2 x R_keeper x C_load, i.e. hundreds of
microseconds, proportional to load), so it cannot be what the reference
library measured either -- its disable tables are exactly load-independent,
their seven load entries differing only by a 1 fs monotonicity epsilon.  What
is measured here instead is the decay of the cell's own output drive current
to half its enabled value, timed from `TE_B`'s 50 % crossing.  The ratios are
tightly clustered (1.46-1.53 on every rise arc across all six cells, both
footprints and all three drives), which is what a consistent
criterion-to-criterion offset looks like rather than noise.

The disable ratios are the only figures in this fragment that fall outside the
2.2-3.2 band for a reason other than a property of the device transform.

## Gaps and caveats

1. **The disable arc uses a documented non-standard criterion** (above).  No
   defensible voltage-threshold measurement of it exists for a purely
   capacitive Hi-Z load; the number reported is a real, reproducible ngspice
   measurement of driver turn-off, not a fabricated value, but it is not the
   same quantity the thin-oxide library reports and the two should not be
   compared numerically.
2. **Disable `rise_transition` / `fall_transition` repeat the delay table.**
   After the driver lets go the output does not traverse a slew, so there is
   nothing to measure.  The thin-oxide library does exactly the same -- its
   disable `cell_rise` and `rise_transition` tables are identical value for
   value.
3. **Disable tables are constant along the load axis.**  That is by
   construction (the criterion is load-independent) and it is the same
   behaviour the thin-oxide library shows.  No synthetic epsilon was added;
   `verify_lib.py` check 4 accepts a constant series.
4. **Enable delays go negative** at the slowest slews and lightest loads
   (down to -2.3 ns).  Measured, monotone along the load axis, and kept
   as-is; `verify_lib.py`'s own docstring notes the same effect for
   combinational arcs at slow input slews.  The thin-oxide library clamps
   these instead.
5. **`einvn` enable/disable fall arcs are not emitted** (only `_rise`
   groups), mirroring the thin-oxide counterparts exactly.  The `einvn` enable
   fall arc is not simulated at all; the disable fall arc is measured (it
   shares a deck with the rise arc) and is recorded in `measured.json` but not
   emitted.
